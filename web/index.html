YOUR FIRST SESSION

From footage to a better training plan.

  1. Prepare the matchup. Choose the expected opposition shape, your match objective and your squad's strengths. Compare the recommendation and its tradeoffs.
  2. Bring a clear clip. Wide footage works best. For aerial footage, use overhead mode and sample two distinct shirt colours from the frame.
  3. Calibrate the view. Mark four pitch corners or the centre-circle extremes. Use precise coordinates if you prefer keyboard entry. Save extra landmarks after camera changes.
  4. Review the evidence. Scrub through the pitch map and match frame. Select a flagged moment, then decide whether it was a flaw, an intentional movement or uncertain.
  5. Make it useful. Coaches can plan the suggested exercise. Players can use the individual cue. Save notes and export the review for the next session.
A spacing warning is a prompt for discussion. Correct calibration, reliable detections and enough visible players are needed for useful measurements.
